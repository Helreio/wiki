Unless it results in death, damage isn’t permanent. Rest combined with treatment can restore a creature’s hit points and help it get better.

### Death

Massive Damage can kill you instantly. When damage reduces you to 0 hit points and there is damage remaining, if the remaining damage equals or exceeds your maximum hit points you instantly die.

### Dropping to 0 hit points 
When a character hits 0 hit points, they are considered *[[Conditions#On Death’s Door|On Death’s Door]].* They fall prone and are *[[Conditions#Incapacitated|Incapacitated]]* (See Condition). 
	At the start of their turn they will do a [[Saving Throws#Death Roll|Death Roll]] against a DC of 10, on a failure they will take one *[[Hit Points#Grievous Wounds|Grievous Wound]]*, additionally any damage taken in this state will add one _Grievous Wound._ 
	At six *Grievous Wounds* the character will die. Any healing will end the condition or a Nat 20 on a _Death Roll._ You will no longer be *[[Conditions#Incapacitated|Incapacitated]]* but you retain all the *Grievous Wounds* accumulated. On a Nat 1 you will take two _Grievous Wounds._

An ally's _Medicine (Intelligence) Check,_ DC 12, will stabilize you and end *[[Conditions#On Death’s Door|On Death’s Door]]* condition, but you will remain *[[Conditions#Incapacitated|Incapacitated]]* for 1d12 min. If they fail the *Check*, you will gain a one *[[Hit Points#Grievous Wounds|Grievous Wound]]*.

*Grievous Wounds* are reduced at the rate of one per _Long Rest_ and completely after a _Full Rest_ with the proper care.

### Grievous Wounds 

|                   |                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 Grievous Wound  | *[[Conditions#Fatigued\|Fatigued]]* until a Short Rest.                                                           |
| 2 Grievous Wounds | Disadvantage on _Skill Checks._                                                                                   |
| 3 Grievous Wounds | Disadvantage on _Attack rolls_ and _Saving Throws._<br><br>Gain a temporary injury for 1d4 weeks (flavor)         |
| 4 Grievous Wounds | _Hit point_ maximum halved (rounded up)                                                                           |
| 5 Grievous Wounds | Gain a permanent injury (-2 to Strength, Agility, Intelligence or Charisma Score, roll 1d4 for the stat affected) |
| 6 Grievous Wounds | Death                                                                                                             |

### Monsters and Death

Most GMs have a monster die the instant it drops to 0 hit points, rather than having it fall *[[Conditions#Unconscious|Unconscious]]* or be *[[Conditions#Incapacitated|Incapacitated]]*.

Mighty Villains and special nonplayer characters are common exceptions; the GM might have them follow the same rules as player characters.

### Knocking a Creature Out

Sometimes an attacker wants to incapacitate a foe, rather than deal a killing blow. When an attacker reduces a creature to 0 hit points with a melee Attack, the attacker can knock the creature out. The attacker can make this choice before the attack roll. The creature falls *[[Conditions#Unconscious|Unconscious]]* and stable with 1 hit points.

### Temporary Health

Spells, Features and Abilities confer temporary hit points to a creature. Temporary Health aren’t actual hit points; they are a buffer against damage, a pool of health that protects you from injury.

When you have temporary health and take damage, the temporary hit points are lost first, and any leftover damage carries over to your normal hit points.

Unless a feature that grants you temporary hit points has a Duration, they last until they’re depleted or you finish a *Short Rest*.

Temporary hit points do not stack. When two instances grant you temporary hit points, only the higher amount is applied to you.
