### I.

The arcane sigils, carved into the old stone, perturbed the air under the church. Even [[Darrion Goddard|Darrion]] couldn't shake off the feeling of uneasiness as he stepped onto the dais.

“May Kalydra’s Light guide us to fulfill our purpose” said Darrion—mostly to himself. The two wardens accompanying him nodded with a solemn expression. Checking their supplies, he noticed two vials of holy water, five thuribles worth of sooth and one thurible of powered silver.

“This is all we could gather in such a short notice, Lightbearer” said [[Mathew]], bowing his head. Few words ever passed his lips. Darrion kept one satchel and handed the other to the Līhteran, while holding his gaze for a moment.

[[John]], the other warden, prepared all the rations needed for the treacherous journey ahead, while [[Elairn]], the [[Church Hierarchy|Līhteran]], proceeded with the ritual, opening the veil. The liturgy spoken in the [[Old Azakian|old azakian]] dialect scraped Darrion’s mind, instilling a sense of vertigo.

The dais rumbled and groaned as the sigils gathered Light. A faint shimmer was spreading across the blackened cobblestone. No less than a moment later, a thick mist enveloped their forms. The air was foul, filled with decay and iron. It took a moment for them to adjust. Even Darrion had a hard time breathing in the stench, he who had been through the veil a few times before. Only the Līhteran was unbothered. He surveyed the surroundings with a stoic expression while lighting the thurible. The twilight sooth simmered with a dull glow, masking their presence. It was the only thing keeping the restless nightmares at bay. Under their feet an ancient trail marked the path, white pebbles littered every few steps like breadcrumbs.

Darrion watched the stars with a soldier’s focus, eyes locked on the seven points of pale light flickering in the sky like dying hearts. One always flickered more than the others—a bad omen, he reckoned. The Līhteran claimed they were on course. He spoke about tethering paths and fate with calm certainty. But Darrion had noticed, since they passed the veil, the man’s shadow slipped across the ground in a direction his body did not follow.

After a while they entered a patch of gray trees. Their shape was twisted by unseen winds, their branches devoid of leaves twirled together in unnatural ways towards the ground while trunks stood suspended up above. They stopped at a small clearing, devoid of vegetation. Old carved markings adorned the trunks at the edges, Het’nor’s sigils of protection, made by a previous expedition. “We shall rest. From here on, peril awaits us.” Darion nodded towards a shadowed path stretching into the gloom.

They unpacked their food in silence. Elairn and Mathew walked the clearing’s edge, retracing the sigils and infusing them with Light while Darrion sat cross-legged on a boulder, loosening the straps of his gauntlets.

“Lightbearer,“ said the younger warden, “Is it true you slew three koshmera by yourself?”

Darrion looked at John, sighed softly, and said, “I had help, the brave souls who fought the creatures before I arrived. I could strike true, if only for their sacrifice. I only regret that I couldn't save more brothers.” John reminded him of his son—green eyes, bright with hope.

John kneeled beside him, offering a small bowl of stew, faint steam curling in the air.

“You should eat, Lightbearer,” John said, voice low. “The road ahead will demand your strength.”

Darrion accepted the wooden bowl with a nod. “And yours, brother.”

The warden smiled faintly, stirring his own bowl. “Faith is the only path worth walking. Besides… if I fall here, it will be where the Light shines brightest.”

Darrion allowed himself a tired smile. “The brighter the light—”

“—the longer the shadow,” John continued, eyes lost in the fireless gloom. “I have heard you say it before.”

They ate in the stillness that followed, bitterness clinging to Darrion’s tongue. The taste was wrong, more so than any of his past journeys. John’s gaze lingered on him as he swallowed the first mouthful. “Rest well Lightbearer. May the Light… protect us,” the young warden murmured.

The wardens had fallen asleep soon after they ate. The first journeys are always the hardest, he thought. Soon the wardens began singing in their sleep. Low, wordless tones, layered like a dirge. It wasn’t the language of the liturgy. Darrion recognised nothing of the Light in it. And yet he found himself humming with the strange melody—before he bit through the inside of his cheek and tasted blood, snapping himself out of the stupor. His heart throbbed, an uneasy weight settling in his gut, one that had nothing to do with faith.

Darrion caught the grimace on Elairn’s face, the Līhteran’s lips twitching as if trapped between words and silence. The cold did little to stop the slow bead of sweat tracing his temple. His fingers clenched in a gesture, one to ward off unseen evil. For a fleeting moment, he thought he heard his own name whispered from the ever so slowly shifting trees. Darrion’s grip tightened; the leather scabbard biting into his palm. The trees around the clearing shifted, almost imperceptibly, their twisted branches curling closer as if leaning in to listen. He glanced at the stars above. The sea of endless black felt oppressing, almost smothering the seven pale lights within. Their light seemed further away than before. Darrion drew a deep breath and told himself it was the Weft weaving tricks… yet the whispers persisted, seeping around his thoughts like smoke, promising something lost, yet within reach.

After the wardens broke camp, Darrion rose, his shoulders stiff. He stepped beyond the clearing, leaving the protection sigils behind. Shadows and whispers clung to him like tendrils, guiding his steps. Elairn fell back slightly, keeping watch, unease coiling tight in his chest at the Lightbearer’s unwavering faith.  

  

Elairn kept to the edges of their group, cloak drawn tight against the ever-shifting air of the Veiled Weft. The stars above remained unchanged, but the shadows beneath them moved like tides around stone. 

Darrion stood in front, whispering into the dark. Not prayers. Elairn knew prayers, he’d been raised on the verses of flame and ash. These were... murmured fragments. One-sided conversations with someone unseen, someone unanswered. 

In between steps Darrion always seemed to hear something back. He would pause mid-whisper, cock his head as if listening to a distant lullaby, then nod as though receiving instructions, orders, even.

The wardens were growing afraid. Mathew had confessed that Darrion had stared at him throughout the night without blinking. Elairn had seen the rot behind men’s minds before, lost travelers led by illusions, star-maps that rewrote themselves in ink and blood. But this was more dreadful. Darrion was not lost, he was following something, and worse, he believed it was holy.

Elairn gripped the silver-threaded compass hidden in his sleeve, thumb brushing the etched sigil of the last true path. He didn’t know if he could find the way back anymore, but he knew one thing with certainty. They were no longer following the stars. They were following Darrion. And Darrion was listening to something that did not belong to the light. 

During a short rest, Elairn burnt the piece of parchment he had found hidden within the sooth. The hastily scribbled words still echoed in his mind. When he returned from the bushes his brows twisted into a frown as he noticed his companions. Darrion was on one knee, eyes closed, taking a vigil while John was eating sour bread. Matthew had vanished, even his tracks were nowhere to be seen. Elairn felt a knot tighten in his stomach, unease crawling up his spine like icy fingers. The Weft pressed closer, shadows stretching and twitching at the edges of his vision. Every instinct screamed that something had claimed Mathew, yet he dared not speak it aloud. The air smelled of wet earth and decay, but beneath it lingered something bitter, something deliberate, waiting. He swallowed the lump in his throat, pushing down his unease and steeled himself for the inevitable.

  
  

Darrion hadn't eaten in two days since he entered the shallow bog; her calling alone was enough to sustain him. She whispered to him even in his waking moments, threading through the spaces between breaths. The Daughter of Ash, her voice silk over bone, wove itself through his thoughts with a certain familiarity. It had to be her—the one from his visions. Briefly one of the stars brightened, just enough to know he was on the right path.  

“Soon” he told himself. Soon he will claim her back to the Light. 

Then muffled voices whispered. They said his armor hung loose. That his words had thinned with each passing hour. That the Light had turned away from him. That his son lay dead in a nameless grave. That the others would betray him. Yet he did not falter. Darrion’s faith was akin to fire, unyielding and consuming. He needed to find her. 

Darrion’s steps faltered as sudden emptiness pricked at the edges of his mind. Mathew should have been here, just behind him, steady and silent as always. The ragged breaths within the eternal night were slow and uneven, but one breath, one presence, had vanished. The muck felt thicker, guilt nibbling at his resolve. Where Mathew ought to have been, the shadows were twisting and pulsing with cruel intent mocking him. 

“When did the Weft claim him?” he grumbled. The others kept quiet, only the relentless whispers disturbed the silence. 

“May he rest his soul.” he said with a deep sigh and pushed forward through the filth.

The Daughter of Ash waited. Nothing else mattered.

### II.

Darrion's mumbling echoed within the eternal night. The weight of his sanctified armour dragged at him, briars clutching each step, like grasping hands. His balance wavered, but his faith did not. The sword, gripped tight, carved a path towards Her through the undergrowth. John followed in his shadow. 

Ahead, a small, broken church leaned crooked above the slanted trees. Shards of glass caught the starlight and shimmered like a shifting grin—half a welcome, half a warning.

Darrion approached cautiously, lighting a torch. Each step through the gnarled roots and tilted stones made the air heavier, thick with the scent of earth and something foul beneath. At the threshold, he paused. The flame fought against the oppressive air and flickered twice before sputtering a faint spark, weak, like a dying man. The lean of the church above seemed to press down on him. The entrance had a deep hollow groove carved into the ground and it seemed like something heavy was dragged inside. The unnatural stillness watched him as he approached.

“May Kalydra protect,” he whispered while sneaking past the nartex. A few strands of silken webs drooped around the room. Inside, candles had bled wax like pale ichor on the rotten floorboards, pooling in twisted patterns. In the centre of the chamber sat a dark metal coffin. Embossed on the lid was Savr'ya’s sigil, pulsating with a pale glow. The Light was fading. He surged forward towards Her, fingers searching at his waist for the holy water and powdered silver, heart hammering in his chest. Before he could reach the coffin, he realized the leather bag with the silver was gone. He glanced back towards where John should have been. His lips parted to call for the other pouch but only a strangled croak escaped. Thin silks glistened in the candlelight, tightening around Darrion’s throat. He spun, eyes darting around for John, but the room was empty; only a faint draft stirred his tarnished cloak in the deafening silence. The feeling of despair seized him, heavy and suffocating. He was utterly alone. 

Writhing tendrils beneath the walls cracked the plaster. With a sharp hiss a wave of small spiders emerged and scurried on the ceiling and walls. Their plump bodies held at the end a bald head devoid of features; only six beady eyes marked where their face should be. 

They descended upon Darrion like the sea, skittering all over his body, like the roar of a landslide made flesh. The overwhelming tide was relentless. His resolve wavered in his chest, and where the Light faltered, the ground bled into an ocean of crimson depths. He pushed against it, forcing himself upwards. Darrion’s head rose above the thick crimson water, fear twisting in his stomach. His lungs burned while the world around him drowned in crimson waters. 

A flurry of limbs, gore and threads tightened around his legs, pulling him down to the depths. Darrion struggled to unsheath his longsword. His strikes were weak and shallow. The fleshy mass kept him bound under the surface. While his sight darkened at the edges, Darrion touched the parchments of absolution affixed to his pauldron. The sigils inscribed hummed with a dim glow before radiating a surge of Light. Feeling the presence dissipating, he swam, piercing the surface. The foul air filled his lungs as his heart throbbed in his ears. Within 15 paces, the water became crystal clear. Looking around, Darrion spotted a small light on the shore. Upon reaching the shore, the light started to dance and flicker between the trees, beckoning him. The armor was drenched and heavy and the liturgies inscribed were missing. The taint had left its mark. It had fulfilled its purpose, protecting him from corruption. Darrion unbuckled the straps with a trembling hand, letting the pauldrons fall first with a dull thud, then the breastplate followed. He dropped to one knee, a brief moment of respite as he murmured a prayer to Kalydra. 

After a moment, Darrion opened his eyes. The small light had disappeared and the same thin webs hugged the gnarled trees like silver threads upon a loom. A figure approached from the edges of the clearing. Its movements were unnatural, almost seeming to imitate life. But he was ready—one fell swoop severed its strings and it fell like a broken doll on the ground. 

“Show yourself! Your petty tricks do not work anymore!” he bellowed.

Darrion stood resolute, while the mangled body sprawled on the ground. Mathew’s lifeless eyes stared at him as a seven foot shadow grew behind the trees. A female’s head revealed itself from the shadows. Its porcelain skin sent a tinge of uneasiness down Darrion’s spine. The creature clicked its tongue observing its prey with a sardonic smile creeping on its face. The koshmera lunged forward, its spider body quick for its massive size. 

Darrion stepped to the side, deflecting the leg aimed at his head, but another one pierced his shoulder. The pain spread through his body like fire. He bit his tongue, then spat blood in its face, momentarily blinding the koshmera. He leaped forward, tightening his grip on the sword. 

His blade bit deep, sparks dancing where steel met chitin. The creature reeled, shrieking, its limbs thrashing in the dim light. He pressed the attack, each strike heavier than the last, his breath ragged, his vision focused. With a swift upward strike he managed to cut one leg cleanly off, pushing the creature a few paces back. 

Then the ground rippled beneath him. At first it was mud, but in the next heartbeat it darkened, flowing red, pooling around his boots. Darrion staggered but did not falter. The tide lapped against his greaves, cold as death, yet the monster still loomed above him, cocking its head with a grin. A light drizzle fell from above hitting their forms and tinging everything red. 

He raised his sword, the crimson filth dragging at every motion, as though the Weft itself conspired to drown him mid-strike. Each swing hissed as it struck droplets of crimson rain. Still he fought, wadding deeper, his resolve burning like a roaring fire. Her faint voice barely whispered in between his breaths, carrying him forward to the Light. 

The koshmera heaved and it belched a coil of threads catching Darrion off guard. The silk binded him, his arms and legs were stuck together. The creature approached, licking its lips in anticipation of its victory.  

“Kalydra, crown my blade in your wrath!!” Darrion howled as the sword lit ablaze. The radiant fyre purged the silks binding him and illuminated the abomination. With a sudden slash he sent a wreath of golden flames toward the creature, searing its flesh. The koshmera wailed and recoiled backwards, and before it could regain its balance, Darrion rolled to its side and plunged the blade deep within its torso. A dozen voices filled the air, shrilling in old forgotten tongues. 

“May the Light cleanse your ashes. Burn!” said Darrion, as he twisted the blade and unleashed the Light. The koshmera stumbled away while violently thrashing as the radiant fyre burned its tainted soul. It fell with a deep thump on the crimson ground. 

Darron sighed in relief as he clutched the wound on his shoulder. He spat on the ground and a nervous chuckle escaped him. He moved closer to Mathew’s crumpled body, inspecting its features more in detail. A narrow puncture marred his chest, a slit no wider than a thumb, right above his heart. Terror twisted Mathew’s face, his last breath was one of surprise and recognition. Darrion kneeled down and retrieved the suncatcher around Mathew’s neck, a reminder for the one he lost and was led astray. 

The air thickened and a cold wind embraced the Lightbearer, reshaping the world around him. This time Her voice beckoned him back and his Light did not waver.   

Darrion felt the rough stone slabs of the church against his back. As he opened his eyes, the metal coffin lay beside him. The candles around the room flickered with crimson light and shadows frolicked on the walls, their limbs bending and twisting in unnatural motions, performing unspoken rituals.  

“Lightbearer?” The sudden voice took him by surprise. John ran to Darrion’s side while reaching behind his back. 

“Holy water, Lightbearer.” The words came strained, his hand extending the glass vial as if it carried more than liquid inside. 

Darrion glanced at his brown eyes and nodded. He rose to his feet with a pained groan. His fingers gently curled around the extended hand. 

“Thank you, brother,” he spoke solemnly. 

Darrion’s expression hardened as he crushed the vial in John’s hand. Flesh blackened and sizzled. Yet John did not flinch—only smiled, lips twisted in contemptuous  defiance, He no longer looked human. Darrion’s blade came in a sweeping arc that unraveled him, splitting cloth, flesh and bone, as though he were a puppet stitched together with thread and smoke. 

The body struck the stone with a hollow thud, its features sagging, sliding off the skull like molten wax. The brand upon John’s hand  flickered and dissolved, heresy unmade. The silhouettes lining the walls stilled, they watched motionless until the last flame bled out and left the room in breathless dark.

  
  

### III.

The church was silent. Only the wind spoke, pushing through the broken windows, carrying the sound of distant bells outside… dull, erratic, mocking him. 

Darrion dropped the sword, his wounded shoulder gushing pale blood. He could feel the blight spreading more with each breath. He lumbered to the edge of the coffin. Lifting the suncatcher from underneath his tunic and laid it on Sav’rya’s sigil. With the last fragments of his Light he renewed the wards, prolonging the invocation protecting Her. He pulled at the iron chains hoisting the coffin of the altar. 

“This is my burden. My duty. My sin” he muttered, dragging the coffin across the old stone. Each pull tore through his strength until even the sound of iron carving stone seemed to fade. 

Outside, the sky awaited him. Darrion squinted as his eyes were assaulted by the color of burnt parchment filled with streaks of sickly purple clouds. The countless days of eternal darkness took their toll. He looked up, searching for the seven pale stars and their guidance. None were in sight, they had been smothered by the Weft. 

The churchyard stretched empty before him, the graves sunken and names worn away. Only one was freshly dug, and it had his name carved into the weathered tombstone. 

Each step felt heavier, each breath borrowed. The burden Darrion carried rattled the chains in his wake, biting into the tough ground singing with a screech of metal against bone, a litany of lost faith.

 His duty compelled him forward, even if the Light faltered, his will remained resolute. Ahead lay the dead mire with twisted shrubs, pale reeds and pools of tainted crimson flanking him in a quiet, suffocating embrace. The bog clung to him with clammy fingers, leaching the heat, slowly from the sundered flesh of his aching shoulder. 

The absence of Her voice gnawed at him, a quiet more cruel than any voice. Even the Weft’s whispers were gone, leaving him with a fetid stench of rot to keep him company. 

With each passing hour, he discarded the remaining pieces of his armor, each plate and strap strained his body as much as his dwindling flame. Despair wrapped around him like snakes coiling through his limbs, pressing his knees deeper into the black mire.

At last, he saw an old gnarled oak towering above like an obelisk awaiting the condemned, a charred trunk breathing faint wisps of mist in spite of the still gloom. 

Darrion let the coffin rest against the trunk and he slunk down on the roots, drawing ragged breaths. A moment of respite. The burden was far greater than he had imagined. A hollow fire was still smoldering beneath his ribs, a pale echo of the golden Light he once carried with pride.  

 A chill brushed his neck, and every instinct flared in warning, muscles coiling against an unseen presence. He whispered a fragmented prayer to Kalydra, the words tasting like ash and rust, and he felt the coffin stir. He saw John again, sitting on the coffin as though nothing had happened. John’s lips moved without sound and blood poured from the seam in his neck, dripping on the coffin’s lid. Each drop corroded the metal like acid while something stirred within Darrion’s veins. Hot ire boiled through him. His smite came swift; the last ember of his Light surged along the [[Belostyl|belostyl]] dagger, setting it ablaze. The white flame poured into the rent, shredding its strings with a hiss of unseen shrills. The smell of burnt hair and iron filled his lungs, chipping away at him even further. Doubt and worry pooling deep inside his belly like tar.  

Darrion’s hand, slick with ichor, brushed the cold metal. Beneath his touch, a faint pulse. The daughter or his own, he could no longer tell. Around him the world warped like heat above the sea; he stood atop a mound of ashen sand, only the oak remained steadfast, a lone sentinel in the gray. 

“The stars are gone, [[The High Heavens|Het’nor]]. Wisdom or damnation—it matters little. Show me the path I must take” he murmured, eyes fixed where the pale sky bled into the dunes. 

He rose, dragging his feet, and slung the chains over his shoulder. A dull ache throbbed where the wound never closed. He trudged forward through the winding slopes for what felt like days until he arrived at a hollow made of jagged stones. A maw of broken teeth cradling a half buried dais, flanked by two arches worn smooth by wind. 

Darrion frowned at the remnants, a long forgotten reliquary within the emptiness. A memento of the first age he reckoned, a god unmade, forgotten even by the First Flame. He stood still, listening, tracing the faded carvings with his gaze. Then it came. A presence bore into his mind, deep, unrelenting, and then he heard it, a few stones skittered on the ground. A man clad in ornate armor, etched with the sigil of his order touched the tenebrous arches with reverence.

“By Kalydra’s will… ” His hand froze on the pommel the belostyl dagger. Emerald green eyes glared at Darrion through the helm’s visor. The cold and unrelenting presence now given form. 

Darrion’s shoulders slumped slightly under the weight of the burden and the lingering ache. He spoke, voice dry but firm.

“I am Darrion Goddard, Lightbearer, Kalydra’s Red Hand. You bear our mark upon your breastplate. Speak brother, state your name and purpose”. His  eyes narrowed at the helm’s slit, dreading the response.
