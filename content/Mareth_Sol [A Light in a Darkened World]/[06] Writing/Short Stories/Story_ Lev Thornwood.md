
### I.

“Fuck this! ..” grunts Lev, looking at the broken handle that used to be a shovel, only a moment ago. 

Greg looks at him with a sly smile.

“Breaking our back only for a few coins, ey? We should ask for more. He owes us better pay for this kind of work. ” 

Lev glares at him with disgust. “Maybe I will deliver two bodies, instead of one, if you don't keep to yourself." Greg's eyes dart down, his innards shiver at the intensity of Lev’s words.

A distant rumble breaks the tension. "A storm is nearing" mumbles Lev in a sour tone, still eyeing the other man. Hard work tonight, he ponders while noticing white knuckles around the battered handle. Exhaling slowly, tempering the storm within, he gestures with a nod to continue digging.

A light drizzle outlines the shape of a cart being pushed by the two cloaked figures through the small street. 

A darkened sky looms over a squared house at the end of the alley. It awaits their arrival. A rusted sign in the shape of a cup flanked by two snakes dangles precariously above the door.

A soft toll escaped the misshapen bell as it was being pulled. 

"Yess.." a croaky voice answers from behind the wooden door while an eye inspects them through the slit. A thin spectacled man prompts them with a discreet movement to enter "Careful now, I do not want mud on my carpet." 

"Of course, Sir," replied Greg meekly.

The doctor watches them unloading the heavy sack with an impish grin. A sharp tongue escapes his lips in anticipation. "Good. As fresh as it can be."  

"Doctor Edwin?” The voice is lost within the now heavy downpour. “How is the cure going?" utters Lev with more vigor this time. “My brother … “ voice trailing off.

The doctor's wispy brows morph into a deep frown for a second, however a contorted smile emerges immediately as he composes himself "Soon…" the word is sharp, cutting deep into Lev's boldness. " Just a few more trials. Until then, give him this." A hand slips down his pocket as he speaks. "Two pills, one in the morning, one in the evening. It should be enough for now". Looking over his shoulder he catches Greg inquiring eyes. A wolf focusing on prey. Soon, but not tonight. I have things to take care of. 

Lev turns towards the doctor and nods in approval while placing the envelope inside the tunic under the waterlogged coat. 

"What about my pay?" Greg squeals with indignation, still keeping an eye on Lev. 

"Here" the doctor drops a pouch, it clangs on the floor. "Now leave, I have work to do."

The men share an icy glance as they part ways. A deep grumble fills the air. Their shapes slowly get lost in the stormy night. 

A grinding sound cuts through the night as Lev opens the front door. Something feels out of place, a few drops of water break the silence, as they fall from the ceiling into a pot. 

"Lev?" a faint voice croaked. "I heard them Lev, I heard them again. They were singing a beautiful song, beckoning me, telling me about the spheres." 

With a gentle hand, Lev touches Theo’s forehead. "You are burning up. Here, I have something for you." Opening the envelope he revealed five red tablets. Trying to keep a caring smile while his hand reaches for the flagon of water. 

"Leeev, they make me feel drowsy.. and the winged men stop singing. Can I do it tomorrow?" Begging with narrow lips and glossy eyes fixed on his older brother.

Lev sigh's for a moment, pondering. "Fine, we’ll do it like this. Half now and half in the morning. Deal?" 

"Yes. " Answered the feeble boy before drinking the medicine. In a few minutes he was fast asleep, not even the windows rattling from the storm bothered him. 

Lev is stirred awake from his shallow sleep by a resounding shrill that pierces the air together with faint rays of morning light. Damm this old rooster, I will murder him one day. While washing his face, some strands of oily black hair drooped over his pale face. “I might need to stay more in the sun, and also cut my hair.” He whispers, not wanting to wake him up. Then proceeds to do chores around the house.  

On his way to get more water, Lev took notice of Onora helping her father at the forge. They get ready to repair a few farming tools, a plow, a couple of shovels and an ax. Onora waves at him with a warm smile. Flustered, he nods awkwardly. 

“How is Theo?” she yells from the other side of the street. “He is holding on, like always.” replied Lev, failing to match her volume. Can't she be more loud than that?, grumbles under his breath while quickening the pace. As usual everybody is already on the field tending to crops and cattle. I heard from the mayor recently that a strange blight is destroying the eastern crops, probably Royse fucked up the Luminous Globes again, that bastard. Lately it has spread. Moreover, even the ancient willow in the square is wilting although they are far away from each other.

A rancid smell fills his nostrils, pushing the thoughts in the back of his mind. With a few steps, he is close enough to peek inside of the well. Water looks stale. With a sigh, dragging his tired body, he manages to walk further to the next well in front of the rundown livery. This one seems good, for now. Lev also procures some apples, bread and a rind of cheese. Happy with the yield of this excursion he starts to hum a song, remembering not her face but the lullaby she sang to him. 
 

Verse 1:

Little one in the dark house,
Did you miss the sun rise?
Why are you crying?
Why all the screams and sighs?
 

Verse 2:

The Sun god awoke with a start,
Angry at the noise from the little one's heart,
"Who has disturbed me?" he exclaimed,
"It's the small one," the heavens proclaimed.

  
Verse 3:

The Sun god's rage began to flare,
As the little one continued to glare,
"Why have you disturbed my sleep,
With your sounds that make me weep?"


Verse 4:

"Come here now," the Sun god spoke,
"Let's settle this and end the choke,
No more crying or screaming, you hear,
For the sun rises to bring you cheer."
 

The little one went to the Sun god's call,
And saw the beauty of it all,
No more tears, no more cries,
Just the warmth of the sun in the skies.
  
  

“I arrived with food.” spoke Lev passing the doorway setting everything on the table. “It's time for your medicine Theo.” 

No answer. Fear drops in the pit of his stomach. “Theo?” the words barely escape his lips, reverberating into the room filling it with uncertainty and dread. The eyes fall on the shape under the covers. Don't do this to me, I don't want to lose you too. 

“Lev?.. I .. “ a croon reaches his ears as his trembling hand pulls the covers. He lays there, still, i…can't. Lev thought while tears started flowing making his brown eyes darker. He clutched the stiff body holding it tight, while a bestial growl escaped his sorrow filled his heart even though he couldnt weep at all years ago on his mother’s deathbed. 

After a couple of hours, within the purple light of dusk, the Presbyter recites the last words as the second moon rises. “May he find peace and solace within the warm embrace of the Allfather, free of pain and suffering.”. 

With one last touch of the course wood he murmurs. “I am sorry I couldn't save you. I failed everyone, dad.. , mom.., even you”. After taking a deep breath, he throws the first shovel of earth hitting the makeshift coffin with a resounding thud meanwhile Onora is weeping for both brothers. 

“He has been laid to rest.” crooked the old man. “My job is done here, unless you are willing to pay.” Spitting on the ground, he continued “May he lay to rest” taking his leave.
