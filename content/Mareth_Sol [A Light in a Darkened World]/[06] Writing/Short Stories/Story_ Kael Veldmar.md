### I.

The sun sank over the mountains, its last light covering the west wing of the Veldmar’s mansion. The music faded, laughter dispersed, and with it the meaningless excitement of the evening. The celebration was not more than an hour ago. Kael could still recall the faces from the feast, their practiced smiles, the way they talked and gestured, unaware their hubris gave it all away. 

He stood alone in his chambers reading his grandfather's grimoire, the only thing that remained after his death. The vellum pages full of old Azakian script held information only a Magus would be privy to. The doors slid open with a sudden agonized groan, drawing his attention away from his thoughts. 

His mother, Eliza, and father, Robert, strode into the room. Thin proud smiles lined their faces. Kael sat up from the small study, greeting them properly. As he glanced past them he saw a small petite frame bound in silk ribbons of a garish crimson. Her steps, stiff and uncertain, were guided by the silk leads his mother held.

A mockery of gentleness.

“Kael, we have brought you a ‘gift’ to mark your twenty-fifth year.” Eliza smiled as she touched Kael's cheek. “Your solitude is depressing; you would do well to learn from your older brother. He is constantly strengthening relations with the other Houses.”

The mirth of hunting, drinking and attempting to bed every highborn woman that catches his eye. Praiseworthy indeed. 

Kael gave a small nod in agreement keeping his derision concealed. 

Eliza pressed on, “Regardless, your father secured this rare gem recently during his travels.” With a small flourish, they guided her forward. 

Her movement was hesitant, as though she expected to be struck. Kael saw her form, the scars and bruises lining her flesh. They were small constellations of pink and violet against her pale skin. The marks of discipline spoke a story of her latest defiance that he couldn't deny. A small thought took root deep within his mind.  

Kael did not speak at first. He remained motionless, his gaze drifting towards the iridescent feathers encircling her neck. With every shallow breath she drew, the silvery plume trembled, catching the glow of the hearth's fire like shards of polished glass. 

A fitting contrast to the gloom. 

He paced a slow circle around her, inspecting the ‘gift’ with a detached expression. 

“Thank you, Mother. Father”, Kael murmured, his voice a seamless mask of filial gratitude. “You always manage to surprise me, especially with such a rare leshy. A Sirin, no less." His parents exchanged satisfied, knowing, glances. 

Robert stepped forward, his boots clinking against the cold flagstones. “It was a hard-fought bargain,” he remarked, his tone tinged with satisfaction. “The merchant had her reserved for another merchant, a mere vassal of House Halvrek, but I knew she would be perfect for your twenty-fifth year on Mareth’Sol. I paid twice the asking price without a second thought.” To Robert, the girl was not a person, but a currency of prestige, an item in a ledger to be allocated and spent. Kael watched them both knowing with certainty that this ‘gift’ was a covert contract in disguise.  

Eliza's smile widened. Though the expression was ostensibly sincere, it felt like a gilded cage. She continued, “I also bring tidings from House Durkhov. The accord has proceeded favorably regarding the additional annex you suggested. The dowry has been sent together with your betrothed; she will arrive on the morrow, as the sun sets. And be given into your care.”

Robert nodded in agreement. “This accord will sustain the Veldmar legacy. Both houses shall thrive upon the bedrock of this union.” His hand came to rest upon Kael's shoulder. “Duty above all,” he intoned. 

Kael slowly lowered his head. The Veldmar tenet was like a thorn lodged in his spine; ever since he had first seen it embroidered on their banners, he had despised it. 

“Duty above all.” his voice a flat reflection as he met his father’s eyes. 

Duty? The grasp of the dead, strangling the future… 

His mother embraced him one last time before they withdrew, closing the doors behind them. A final sound of wood on stone plunged the chambers into a sudden silence. The room was quiet, thick with tension.

Kael felt her gaze following him as he traversed the room, returning to his old, worn leather chair. He retrieved the grimoire from the desk and resumed his studies, pointedly ignoring her presence. With every page he turned, he saw with the corner of his eye; her demeanor slightly shifting. After the first ten pages he could feel her ease up, page by page. The rigid line of her shoulders began to soften. When he reached the 38th page her bright blue eyes held more confusion than uncertainty. She began to restlessly fidget with the fabric of her gown and a few pages later she finally spoke up. 

“M..Master?...” her voice faint, barely a whisper. 

I thought it would take longer. Kael suppressed a faint smile before lifting his head. He fixed her with a look, narrowing his eyes slightly. 

“Is there a reason—you're disturbing my sanctum?” He spoke in a flat tone. 

The girl immediately averted her gaze, unable to meet his inquisitive amber eyes. Her body visibly shuddered at the intensity of his stare. After a few moments she took a deep breath, her chest heaving under the weight of her gown.

“I..I have been instructed to… be of service…” she trailed off, her voice tapering off while stealing a frantic glance at him. Kael remained still, his expression unreadable as he simply watched her. 

“You are not worthy to serve me, I have others that perform for me with far more… finesse.” Kael tapped a single finger against the desk, a rhythm like a heart beat. “I am not interested in a ‘prize’ chosen for me. Other nobles used goods do not entice me. ” 

Behind the veil of silver hair, her lip twitched in a flash of indignation. It lasted for a mere fraction—but Kael saw it. 

Her head tilted, the feathers on the back of her neck stood up while her hair parted just enough to reveal a single eye simmering with quiet anger.

“I am sorry Master. I'm sorry I have been spoiled by three other nobles before. Maybe if I could have been caught later you would have been my first.“ 

Kael let out a chuckle. “Three other nobles?” he pretended to ponder for a moment. “Why would, not one but three nobles let a rare little bird like you fly away?—Your song has matured before its time, hasn’t it?.”

The girl flinched. Her wide eyes fixated on him before darting towards the tall windows. 

Kael stopped tapping. A thin smile tugged at the corner of his lips while his finger moved above the sigils on the page in front of him, tracing a slow pattern into the air.

“You can attempt to run, but there will be consequences.” He said, his voice betraying a faint amusement at her panic. “Make your choice, little bird. Do you fly into the night, or do you use your song like before?” 

The girl clenched her teeth, eyes narrowing at Kael’s unbothered demeanor. A sharp intake of air rattled her throat. The nascent hum deep within her chest began to resonate, making all the glass in the room shudder. As she parted her lips, the magic flowed off her tongue like honey, thick and viscous, weaving thin purple threads.

The magic coiled through the air, lashing out towards Kael. Yet, he did not flinch; he did not even blink as the threads hissed past his cheek. Instead, his hand remained steady, his finger continuing to trace the sigils on the page. His thin, calculated smile split into a wide grin. 

She pushed further, her defiance turning into a weapon. The girl began to weave ancient, hollowed words into the melody, intertwining together with low hums. The feathers on her neck faintly shimmered with primordial power. The sound, now a lament of an age long passed, bled into the chamber, turning the magical threads a bruised violet. They became thick and heavy; snake-like tethers that pulsated with her rhythm. A rhythm of inconsolable grief that forced men and women alike to buckle to their knees. 

Kael winced as the song shifted. The Lullaby of Woe was clawing the frayed edges of his mind. For the first time, his fingers faltered, as the pull against his will grew stronger by each passing the moment. 

He grit his teeth, the wide grin strained. He forced himself to endure the pain. To will himself through it. 

Kael’s free hand gripped the edge of the study, wrapping his fingers against the sharp edge of the wood. An anchor to steady himself against the overwhelming tide of purple lytel swirling around him. 

The girl’s expression twisted into a sardonic smile at the sight of him struggling; for a fleeting moment she looked certain of her triumph. But the victory was short lived. 

Kael leaned forward, his amber eyes locking onto hers, flickering with resurgent power. His fingers touched the page with a final tap, then pointed directly at her. 

The air around the girl warped. The unnatural pressure forced her song down, dragging the melody several octaves lower. The dissonance shattered the rhythm, fracturing the Lullaby into a low, staccato whisper. A jagged, broken dirge. 

A few droplets of blood stained the wood of his study. Kael touched his upper lip, his fingers coming away wet and warm. He stood slowly, the crimson still flowing as he kept concentrating on the spell. 

He walked towards her, each step strained and heavy. With every step he took, she tried to push harder, her magic clashing against his, trying to force the Lullaby through. 

As he came to a halt in front of her, he spoke.

“Enough.” 

Kael made a sharp gesture in the air. The chamber plunged into silence. The air around her form vibrated, only a faint hum remained of her song.  

Her eyes went wide with terror. The girl began to struggle frantically against her restraints, her fingers fumbling to untie the silk ribbons binding her hands. A final desperate act of rebellion.  

He watched her as a thin line of blood trickled down his chin. In a swift motion, his hand shot forward and caught her by the throat, stifling the remnants of the song. The silence was deafening; interrupted only by her croaks. The thrill exhausted him more than he expected; his laboured breathing was proof of that. 

Kael composed himself with a deep breath, and spoke, a tinge of excitement in his voice. 

“You might be of use after all.” 

He reached out, his stained finger tracing a complex sigil directly above her throat. As his lips formed the old Azakian word—Writhghast—his blood mark upon her skin began to simmer. A cold, blue light flared, then sank into her flesh, vanishing completely. 

A moment later, he asked, “What is your name?” His grip loosened just enough for the sound to escape her throat, though his gaze remained fixed on her pupilless eyes.   

“L..Lily..” she managed to croak.  

“Your leshy name.” A sharp edge in his voice. His shadow flickered over her small form, swallowing her.

She averted her eyes, her breath still strained under the pressure of his hold. For a silent moment, she hesitated, but then her shoulders loosened, a visible sign of defeat. 

“My name… S̴̟͌h̴̢̅à̸̩ŕ̴͎i̸͔̾k̸͜͠a̶͚͐…”

Kael nodded, then released her. “You bare my mark, your Lullaby will not work on me anymore.”

She crumpled on to the floor, a sigh of relief. The first true breath under Kael, her new master. 

“Master…” her voice barely above a whisper. Kael raised his brow slightly, gesturing to her to continue. “How can I serve? “  

He pointed to the furthest wall of the chamber, where a massive four-poster bed stood. Its velvet hangings swayed in the faint draft, promising a sweet embrace. A bit of flush came to her cheeks, as she saw the bed. Kael took the ribbon and guided her, when he reached the bed he undid her ribbons, laying her on the covers. Her silver hair was splayed like a halo around her head. Her glossy eyes watched him.
