**Third Age, Year 579
  
A few droplets of rain stung Jim’s brow. Between the itch of his cheap linen and the pinch of his boots, the night watch felt more like a sentence especially together with… Bert. A fat, stubby, little man who is always talking about his sow of a wife, and his little piglets—or how good of a commander he would be in spite of his peasantry. 

“One of those days…” Jim muttered, a feeling of defeat washing over him.

“Ei laddie. Have you heard what happened at the outpost up north, near Har’nan?” 

“What? Do they need you as their captain?” replied Jim in a snarky tone, still watching the horizon where brooding clouds clashed with one another.

Seeing his expression, Bert pondered for a second. “Maybe,” he answered with a smirk. “ …after they rebuild it.”  

“What do you mean?” Jim deepened his frown.

“Well, earlier, while I got myself ready for the watch, I overheard one of the messengers talking to the Captain. The Lord Commander of Il’shar couldn't get in contact with the outpost and after that, they sent a detachment to investigate. Apparently they found it destroyed, burnt to the ground, and with no bodies in sight.“

“Eh, isn't it the third incident this year? What are the higher-ups doing? Since they let the dross filth join our ranks everything has been going to shit,” Jim punctuated the sentence with a bit of phlegm as his hand gripped the railing.

“If I could, I would undergo the surgery for a higher position. You know, you also get benefits,“ replied Bert with a glint in his eye. “Better pay, only seven years of mandatory service. Doesn't sound that bad.”   

“If you manage to survive that long. Besides… is losing your mind and soul worth it?” Jim grumbled. His gaze wandered to the western wall of the outpost as Bert continued to trail off on a tangent, like he always did. 

Something was amiss, the air felt suddenly colder. 

“Quiet. Do you see that?” Jim pointed at the thick coils of pale mist rolling up the hill. 

“Mist?” mumbled Bert with a confused frown. “I'll get the chaplain,” he added, while hastily descending the worn-out ladder. Meanwhile, Jim had opened the small leather pouch containing twilight soot and started to fill the censer to the brim. As he reached for the flint in his pocket, his body froze. Strange clacking sounds echoed through the trees, sending chills down his spine. His eyes followed the hunched shapes slowly hobbling at the forest's edge. 

“Hurry!” he begged quietly, tracking their movements as the fat man descended the watchtower’s ladder.
