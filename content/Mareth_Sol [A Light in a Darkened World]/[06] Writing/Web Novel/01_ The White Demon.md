
#### I
White squatted behind a bush, one hand on the bow, while the other brushed against the arrows in his quiver.

Five arrows. I need to make them count.

The cold damp mud was keeping him grounded from the anger rising in his chest. This was the fourth time they enacted this plan. This time was different, they didn't have much time to prepare. Alana gave him the arrows together with three acorns, both imbued with wild Leshen magic. 

White’s amber eyes had been watching the tower at the end of the wooden wall. Inspecting the threads of lytel coming off the warding sigils in blurry strands. They were meant to keep nightmares at bay. But tonight it will be of no use, White was a man, their worst nightmare made flesh. 

 After a few minutes Haroon’s mist crept up the hill through the forest, slowly engulfing the camp together with a deep rhythmic rattling of antlers. 

White noticed a stubby man frantically attempting to climb down the ladder. A moment later a flaming arrow whistled through the air embedding itself into the watchtower. It erupted in wild flames setting it ablaze. The man fell from the ladder and struggled to run away raising the alarm. 

  

This was the signal White was waiting for. 

  

He quickly got on his feet and bolted behind the last trees at the edge of the clearing losing himself within the mist. Shadowy shapes loomed and lurched, a macabre dance intended to confuse their prey. From what Alana told White there were thirty men manning this outpost, a dozen more than the last one. White half smiled wearily, licked his lips before stringing the bow.

Soon, he thought, they will come.

The bell rang, a deep mournful sound. A sound to light a fire in the hearts of soldiers. Alas it was quickly drowned out in the rattling noise.

A minute later the gates cracked open. A dozen men wearing chainmail, fully armed with swords, spears and a few shields. From thirty meters away, White could see them through the mist. His amber eyes were the only gift he possessed. 

The soldiers had weary expressions as they marched outside to defend the camp. White steadied his aim and released one arrow. It pierced through the shield and the man, leaving a gaping hole in the center of his chest. Chaos insured, one was pulling the downed man to safety not realising he was already dead. 

Another arrow parted the mist piercing through the shoulder of another and stopping in the belly of the one behind him. 

  

Three down. White’s fingers twitched toward his quiver, but he pulled back. These scum are not worth any more Leshen arrows.

  

While shadowy illusions distracted them, White dropped the bow into the mud and slunk forward through the mist. He came behind one of the men on the right. Silently unsheathed the axe from his back and delivered a blow to the nape, instantly killing the soldier with a gurgled scream. With catlike moves White moved back low to the ground, his alabaster skin making him hard to spot in the pale mist. The men started to tighten the ranks while yelling for their orders. 

  

Seven more to go. 

  

His body tensed before springing forward, taking another soldier by surprise. White avoided his shield and lodged his axe into the soldier's thigh. He swiftly took the sword from the collapsed man and plunged it into another’s groin. The screams were harrowing, barely heard above the noise. Crimson tinted the misty air, the taste of copper prickling White’s nose.

Suddenly a spear came for his head but missed by five fingers. Haroon’s spell worked flawlessly confusing their senses while for him it was a slight inconvenience. 

A couple more spears came this time. White let go of the sword and rolled to the side, jumping out of the way. He grabbed the shaft of one spear, yanking it free, then he pressed it forward between the gaps in their shields, aiming it at the head of the one leading them. The man knocked the spear to the side in the nick of time leaving only a small graze under his chin.

White dropped the spear and flung himself backwards into the mist. A moment to get himself in a better position. Two of them bolted back towards the gate while the others were trying to discern his position. 

The soldiers moved in a defensive posture, their backs together covering every angle of attack. He watched them, from five meters  away, slowly retrieving his axe from the crumpled corpse.  

  

Your deaths will not be swift, his words, drowned by the cacophony of the rattles mingled with the bell. May Effrie have mercy on your souls, though I will not. 

  

The one wearing a half plate, some lesser noble’s son, had thin strands of light blue lytel around his armor and dark red lytel flowing from his arm into his sword. 

White unhooked a small iron flask from his belt and took a sharp, burning swig. He dropped the vessel into the muck.

To his left, the orange haze of the barracks flared as the timber roared beneath the flames. The fire illuminated him through the dissipating mist. His white hair slick with blood stuck to his face, his lean spindly body made him look like a white fiend caked with dark red. 

White’s amber eyes were affixed to the enemies before him. His expression was blank, but anger burned through his veins. He bolted suddenly forward, picking up his axe from the mud and slid under the soldier, hooking his ankle. The man stumbled to the side, falling over the other soldier dragging them both to the ground. White took a dagger from his boot and stabbed viciously at their exposed legs, carving deep grooves into them, slashing their tendons and veins. A sabaton struck him squarely in the jaw, pushing him off the fallen men. White rolled through the mud still clutching the dagger. Standing up, in front of him the noble was raising the sword for a downward strike. Lips pursed, lytel flew into the sword igniting it ablaze with white flames. 

As the strike came down he spat the bitter liquid upwards, igniting it as it touched the flames. An orange ball of fire took the knight by surprise, blinding him. White moved closer and blocked the nobles hands on his shoulder, stopping the attack short. His dagger scraped against the magically enhanced breastplate and got caught by the rolled edge of his gorget. White’s eyes narrowed, anger flaring, he coiled his hand over the noble’s hands, grabbing the helmet and yanking left to expose the neck. A swift strike later blood washed over the armor, smearing it with red, the noble gurgled silently before crumpling to the ground. His two men squealed in the mud for a while as White parted their heads from their bodies then he sat on the noble’s body, taking a moment to recover. 

  

The mist and the noise was starting to die down, the spell was fizzling out. A hooded scrawny figure approached from the forest. His eyes were two beady pools of darkness, his skin tight over the bones. He looked like a slight breeze would knock him over if it wasn't for the gnarled staff he propped himself with. White eyes picked up the threads of lytel coalescing around the lodes implanted in the man’s arms.  

  

“Took you a while,” said White through raspy breaths. “I got lucky,” White knocked on the bloody breastplate with a sly grin, “a fairly incompetent cocksucker this one.”

  

 “I did not expect the Arcanist and Chaplain to interfere with my work. Alana had messed with them, destroying their wards.” Haroon spoke while looking at the buildings on fire. “Alas, what remains of their men are cornered in the chapel.” The last words left his mouth together with a trickle of blood. He wiped it with his sleeve.

  

“He was cocky enough to go outside of the camp, It was easier to end him here. The chaplain backing him up would have been a more difficult endeavor. Is your sorcery spent?”

  

“I am drained but I can still sling some spells.” 

  

“Good,” said White while unbuckling the straps of the breastplate and stripping the armor from the noble. 

  

Haroon picked the iron flask from the mud. He took a gulp of the oily alcohol and chuckled softly. As he stood there waiting for White to disguise himself with the nobles belongings a piercing whistle cut through the noise. 

  

“She is in position,” said the old man. “We must continue.”

  
  

#### II
Walking through the camp, most of the buildings were reduced to piles of smouldering embers. The breastplate was clinking against the chainmail, it was a bit loose on White’s lean frame. Nonetheless he was walking victorious pompous noble. The heat and smoke kept away from the chapel’s walls. Alana did her part well, now the only remaining thing was to capture the Chaplain. 

His eye did not betray him, the faint light enveloping the building was tainted, a black thread was weaved within the light. 

White was 20 paces away from the entrance, looking behind, the mist had dissipated only some patches of smoke and Haroon’s gray ribbon of lytel was connected to him. 

Three knocks echoed against the heavy iron door. A moment of silence followed. White keen ears discerned movement inside the small church while his nose picked up on the smell of twilight soot and burnt metal.  

  

“I order you to open these damn doors!!” A few moments passed then he continued, “I sustained heavy casualties but the enemy is dead, one of them atleast.” White raised his hand to show the battered head in his grasp. The gnarled head looked eerily similar to his own.  “The others fled back into the forest.”

A small slot slid open, a pair of brown eyes peered suspiciously at White. 

  

“Today, Ma’el shall receive an offering.” White uttered with conviction while pulling the vambrace to show the occult glyph on his skin. 

  

The slit closed and the iron door groaned as it moved. Inside, half a score of men stood at the ready, steel in hand. The one behind the door was a man of faith, his cloak had golden trimmings and the sigil of Kalydra sewn on the back. 

  

“Welcome back Captain. They did a number on us. Besides the men accompanying you, we lost thirteen. A few died by arrows and the rest fighting flames.”

  

  

  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  
  

#### III
The Commissar stood atop a jagged rise of blackened stone, surveying the area. The heavy black coat, adorned with the badge of office, swayed in the gentle breeze. The smell of rain and ash was still strong in the air. 

He glanced over the five men investigating the aftermath while puffs of smoke escaped his lips. Where once buildings had stood, now lay only charred wood and mud. One of the soldiers approached him.

  

“Commissar,” spoke Lieutenant Flint with a gravelly rasp. 

“We have found a fresh grave in the woods nearby. It contains the body of an emaciated leshy boy, a half breed. No other bodies were found.” 

  

“The pattern appears similar to the other incidents, except for the grave. Anything else of note?” 

  

“Yes. A carved wooden stag, a Leshen charm, was in the child’s hand.”

  

Commissar Konnrad raised an eyebrow, “Peculiar. I have no reports of any tribes around these parts, especially of Cernos descent.” A small grin crept at the corner of his mouth. 

We might track him down after all. 

“Let us proceed to the chapel, Lieutenant.” 

  

The small building stood alone among the cinders. The windowless stone walls were untouched by flames, even though the adjacent barracks had been burned to the ground. 

The Commissar lowered his head upon entering the narthex. In the middle of the nave a dark stain seeped through the embroidered carpet. Further away in the apse, close to the back of the chapel, a circle made of branches, animal bones and sinews had been twisted together. A severed hand was strapped to the center of the fetish. 

A mockery in poor taste; I would have used a head. Alas, that's what they deserve with only one exit and no windows, he thought, inspecting the chamber.

  

“Sir, the severed hand bears the taint of corruption,” spoke hooded figure with a rugged broken voice.”The symbol is faded, it has been there for a few years at least." He looked like most of the Arcanists within the military: gaunt, pale and scrawny—a ghost of his former self. 

“It is similar to the ones found at the other incidents, although this one has been tortured.” 

  

“The church is not what it used to be, Savir,” the commissar replied. “The Inquisition is old and senile. They have let their flock scatter without guidance.” 

  

Savir nodded and continued. “There is a hidden space under the altar and signs of makeshift living quarters.” 

The Commissar approached the hatch, wincing in distaste at the stench of sweat, filth, and stale bodily fluids that wafted from within. 

  

“Savir, destroy the chapel. Make sure nobody can find anything in this place,” he ordered. Konnrad took a long drag from his pipe and turned his gaze toward young Lieutenant Flint. 

“Prepare the carriage and the horses. We’ll head for the nearest settlement, Il’shar. They couldn’t have gone far, especially with all that baggage.”

  

“Yes, sir,” replied the Lieutenant, bringing his heels together firmly. 

  
  

#### IV
Six hours had passed since they had reached the proper road. The swaying of the carriage had subsided to a gentle rocking. Arcanist Savir felt a flicker of relief; the nausea had been getting to him. He wished he could have gone on horseback, but the Commissar’s safety mandated that he stay in close proximity. As they drew closer to the town of Il’shar, Savir broke the heavy silence after noticing the Helliumite Lodes in his forearm growing dimmer, the constant thrum fading to a cold ache. 

“Sir, the cimmerian circuits are weaker around the town, the flow of lytel is diminished.” His fractured voice grated on his own ears, but it was a favorable report, nonetheless. 

“Their thaumaturge should be impaired among these hills.”

 “I think you should be able to handle it.”, the Comissar replied with a pleased smile. The subtle praise took him by surprise. He straightened himself. The acknowledgement of his efforts made him steel his resolve; to be of use, no matter the cost. 

The Commissar’s gaze lingered on Savir, gauging his expression.

Even if the lytel is weaker, I will not be a burden, Savir thought, trying to temper his exhilaration as he nodded his head. “I can, sir.”

A few moments passed, and the carriage ground slowly to a halt. The groan of a nearby portcullis was accompanied by the angry shouts of a quarrel. There was a knock on the carriage door, then Lieutenant Flint spoke. 

“Commissar, the watchmen want to confirm your identity, as instructed by Count Erwil. By several accounts from the surrounding area, a seditious group is impersonating high ranking military officers. A few nearby villages were ransacked last week, and several villagers were killed.”

With visible annoyance, the Commissar alighted from the carriage and presented himself in front of Il’shar’s northern gate. His retinue followed closely behind, with Savir leading the way to ensure their protection. Upon seeing the badge of office catching the light, the man in charge hurried to greet Commissar Konnrad, though he kept a wary and disdainful eye on the Arcanist. 

Savir had feared prejudice like this before climbing the ranks, but these days it was nothing he wasn’t used to. 

"Welcome to Il’shar, sir!" the disheveled, forty-year-old man said, saluting with a sharp jerk. He looked as if he might snap his back from holding such a stiff, upright position. “I apologize… for the inconvenience. I was just following the Count’s orders.” he squealed, the words sputtering through missing teeth. 

The Commissar replied with an irked tone, dismissively waving his hand.

“Hail to the Emperor.” 

The dozen guards in front of the gate saluted back, some out of sync. “Hail the Emperor!” 

Undisciplined peasants… Savir would have spat if he wasn't in the presence of his superior.

"Captain, in the past week, was there any suspicious activity in town?" the Commissar asked.

“No, Sir.” 

“Think harder.” 

“Sir…” Beads of sweat trickled down the Captain’s forehead.

“Search the past week’s ledgers. If you don't find anything, find me the ones who took the bribe or I’ll make sure your brigade is sent to Ereš-qi-gal. They always appreciate fresh bodies up there.” The mention of the accursed city sent a surge of uneasiness through the men. Some instinctively clutched their suncatchers—small charms hanging from their necks—seeking protection from the Seven.

The Commissar paused a moment; then continued, “Send word to Count Erwil of my arrival. He should not worry; my visit will be short." His sardonic smile never failed to send shivers down people's spine. “Also, inform the church that Commissar Konnrad requires a līhteran guide back to the capital. Understood, Captain?” The last words, slow and deliberate, cut through the heavy air like a blade.

“Yes, Commissar! I’ll take care of it personally!” squeaked the Captain. 

“Dismissed.” Turning back toward Lieutenant Flint, he nodded and said, “You stay here and make sure they obey. Deal with the rat yourself and join us at the barracks afterwards.” The Lieutenant saluted. His hand dropped to the hilt of his sword as he stalked toward the Captain, whose linen shirt was now drenched with sweat.

After a few short puffs on his pipe, Commissar Konnrad addressed his men. “Let’s get moving. I know a few places where they might be lying low.”

Traveling through the winding, narrow streets, Savir gagged at the pungent smell in the air. His lip quivered in disgust at the boorish inhabitants out on the streets. 

Blast this filth, they're still throwing shit everywhere. Their absurdity is beyond the night. 

After a while, the carriage ground to a halt. This was the last abandoned halfway house in this part of  town and the final one on their list.

Savir peered through the thin red curtains of the carriage. The view brought back the bitter taste of his past struggles, before he joined the military. A run-down, one-story building stood nestled between two cottages, with a suncatcher dangling in the wind. The looming shadow cast by the evening light made it look distorted. 

Something felt off. The lytel felt as if it was being siphoned away.

“A warding sigil, sir, ” Savir said, while looking at his hand. He managed to conjure a small bead of flame on one of his fingertips. “There is enough lytel for our purpose.” The Commissar nodded as he descended the two wooden steps and adjusted the poltka on his head, signaling for the three remaining officers to take their positions. 

  
  

#### V

 The Arcanist wove a sigil in the air; the door shimmered with a dim gray light. After a moment, the wood groaned in protest and the doorframe warped, as an unseen force pressed against it. The door shook violently and was hurled inward, crashing into the room beyond in a spray of splintered oak and dust.

The Commissar stood a few paces back, looking past the ruined doorway. Two of his men surged forward, weapons at the ready, their movements precise and disciplined. Konnrad waited; pipe in hand, tapping it lightly.

Through the gaping hole, he spotted the albino moving swiftly, positioning himself between the advancing men and five terrified children. A spark of interest flickered in his eyes. 

The young man’s burnished amber eyes burned with a tinge of fury, betraying the storm simmering beneath his calm demeanor.

Promising, he mused as he took another puff from his pipe, watching the scene unfold.

The soldier in front hesitated, his attention drawn towards the sobbing children. The pause was enough for the young man to dash towards him. One swift kick and the saber flew from the soldier’s hand. Before he could react, the albino delivered a barrage of blows, leaving him crumpled on the floor, his face a bloody mess. 

The Commissar frowned slightly. He felt it—a shift in the air, pressure dropping as the street outside grew silent. It was the familiar hum of lytel being drawn upon, the cimmerian circuits resonating deep underground. The subtle vibration signaled the Arcanist’s power at work. A thin miasma worked its way close to the floor, enveloping the children, swirling around them like a ghostly shroud.

The second soldier took the albino by surprise, sending both of them sprawling across the room. They crashed onto a table. Splinters erupted into the air as it shattered beneath their combined weight. Soon, the albino’s head was pressed firmly against the cold floor, his breaths coming in labored gasps from the impact.

“If you resist any further, the children will suffer the consequences,” Savir spoke with a thick pessek accent as he entered the room.

The young man was hoisted up and forced to confront the sight of the children lying unconscious on the floor. He clenched his teeth, but complied. The albino was quickly dragged outside and forced to kneel on the grimy street, where the Commissar stood waiting.

Savir conjured ribbons from the cobblestones beneath the albino, wrapping them around his feet and hands, restraining him in place. The Commissar watched as his men withdrew their hands, backing away from the captured man. One soldier tended to the officer with the broken nose, while the other made his way into the house, keeping watch over the children.

With a flick of his wrist, the Arcanist conjured two sigils that flickered above his hand, weaving a barrier of shifting air around them. The albino gasped, his breath shallow, while the world beyond became muffled and distant. He groaned, the sound distorted, as if caught in a whirlwind.

“You are a hard man to find,” Konnrad remarked, packing his pipe with slow, deliberate movements.

“Yet, here we are.” He took a long drag of the pipe, letting the smoke curl upwards and followed with his eyes the hatched pattern it made against the shifting air. “For your crimes against the Azak Empire, your sentence should be execution, in accordance with imperial law.”

“By Rak’ul’s judgment, they deserved it! Their sins—I cannot forgive.” 

The Commissar’s gaze did not flicker; he merely gestured with the stem of his pipe toward the children inside the house. 

“You are blinded by your own fervor. You speak of sins while ignoring what is right in front of you. By the letter of the law, these ‘innocents’ are accomplices. And the Empire does not leave loose ends.”

The albino lowered his gaze to the pavement, a pained expression on his face. The faint hum of lytel filled the silence. After a couple of moments, the Commissar continued. 

“I am willing to overlook your transgressions against the Empire and even spare the children’s lives. There is an orphanage in Arquen, under my jurisdiction, where they will be well cared for. In return, I need you to join the Forty-First Brigade under my watch.” Konnrad leaned a bit closer. 

“You may have survived the battle of Roh’dan, Ghinn Blutaxt, but I will end you right here if necessary.” The Commissar's gaze bore into him. 

“I remember when I first saw you—tearing through enemy lines and breaking their ranks during the counter-offensive. A white fiend indeed.” After taking a deep pull from his pipe, he continued, “It is a pity you are among the last survivors of the Tenth Regiment. They could have accomplished so much more, had they been under my command.”

“Sir, there is a disruption in the flow of lytel,” the Arcanist interjected. “And it is heading toward us.”

A waft of dark mist flooded the narrow street, its coils writhing and clinging to every surface. The warding sphere took the brunt, like a boulder resisting the force of a rushing river. 

The Commissar's face hardened. “How did it pass the warding sigils on the walls? Is nobody in the church doing their duty?” 

“I know what it is. We need cover. Now,” said the albino. 

“Let us get inside, then.” 

The sphere dissolved as the Commissar pivoted to issue orders to his soldiers, his voice barely audible beneath the sudden tolling of church bell.

  

#### VI
Alana leapt as a heavy thud reverberated through the roof where she had stood moments before. The narrow streets below echoed with the sound of shattering windows. Her sharp sense of smell had saved her—there was that pungent odor, a reminder of thunderstorms on the mountain. Strange, rhythmic sounds thrummed in the mist-filled air, and remnant filaments crackled in jagged arcs.

The skittering grew closer, making her shiver. Although the dense mist obscured her vision, her instincts were sharper than ever. The skirmishes and incursions alongside White and Haroon had honed her skills. Shingles cracked under her boots, and wooden beams splintered under the weight of her pursuers. In one fluid motion, Alana pushed off the roof and shot an arrow before landing on the next building. A second later, a satisfying thump echoed as one of the deformed creatures plummeted into the dark mist below. Her worry lessened, if only for a moment.

The distant ringing of the bell slowed her pursuers, giving a brief respite, but the wound at her side stung painfully, warm blood trickling down. She gritted her teeth and kept moving, every breath sharp with the taste of iron.

These abominations will be on my trail. I cannot lose them. Stag’s Mercy! I need to find White—quickly.

After a few more streets, a familiar scent caught her nose—along with a pungent mix of mud, blood and burnt metal. Her instincts flared as she drew upon the charm hanging by her waist. Following the scent through the dimming light, she stopped when she saw the outline of a sphere, still visible through the thickening mist. Three figures moved within, their shapes blurred yet defined. She recognised White’s posture between the others. One of them weaved a sigil, dismissing the orb of wind and let the black mist wash over them. At this realization, fear squirmed in her chest. 

A sullied… 

She watched for a moment, picking her target, the thaumaturge. Her whistling arrow was swift, slicing through the mist like a hawk's cry. However, before it could find its mark, an invisible force deflected it. Alana ducked quickly for cover behind a chimney. Her heart was racing and her muscles were tensing from the exertion. When no retaliation came, she composed herself and moved silently, climbing onto the next roof and slinking into the attic of the orphanage where White had been before.

Peering through a half-opened hatch, she noticed below three soldiers that were frantically barricading the windows and the destroyed doorway. The thaumaturge—the target she had sought earlier—was releasing White from his restraints while the older man stood by the half asleep children.

White retrieved his dagger from the floor and spoke, loud enough for her to hear, "Alana. I need your help. They… are allies. "  

Upon hearing the words she stowed her bow, then descended to the lower floor through the hatch. Alana caught the surprised glances of the soldiers, their eyes widening at the sight of her towering form. Slim and over two meters tall, with silver trinkets adorning her small antlers, marked her as a rare sight—a Leshen, sired by Cernos the White Stag.

“Haroon?” inquired White.

“It took us by surprise. Haroon remained behind. He provided me with an opening to flee. This creature was unlike any other; it was vile, stitched together, part human and part goat. I am so sorry.”

White grunted and gave a short nod. She saw the worry in his eyes, and the realization dropped like a stone into her stomach. The older man watched her with hawk-like eyes while the thaumaturge stole disdainful glances. 

“A koshmera,” White continued, his eyes narrowing as he weighed the implications. 

“Take the children to the basement, Haroon’s ward should mask their presence, but secure the hatch with one of your charms just in case.” 

Alana reached into her pouch and took out a small acorn, the only one she had managed to infuse before the attack. She handed it to White.

“It is not much, but it will be of use,” she said softly. 

White's hand lingered on hers for a moment before he took the charm. 

“How is your wound?” asked White while lowering his eyes to her side. 

Alana remembered the ache, “I’ll heal it as soon as I can,” she responded as her hand touched the jagged wound. “I will wait for your return,” Alana said quietly while holding his gaze.

“Go now, keep them safe. I will find Haroon, if not I will make them pay.” White’s amber eyes hardened, conviction flaring deep within them. 

Alana turned and followed his instructions, she guided the children into the musky, damp cellar. Shortly after the enchantment was set, a thundering boom rocked the foundations of the building, followed by a violent splintering on the floor above. 

Dust cascaded through the floorboards, filling the cellar with a grey shroud. All five children huddled behind her, trembling as they clung to her tightly.

“May we graze with Cernos once again,” she whispered.  

  
  
  

#### VII
The bell’s tolls had ceased. Silence hung in the air, broken only by the ragged breaths of the soldiers standing a few paces away. White’s eyes narrowed, his intuition drawing his gaze to the roof. He heard it then, a faint slinking against the singles. His grip on the worn hilt tightened, with each passing second the cold metal pressed against his palm. 

Three of them, I reckon. He licked his lips in anticipation.

“Commissar, do you have any Twilight Sooth with you?” Said White, eyes locked on the attic hatch as his senses began to prickle with familiar dread.

The Commissar’s expression tightened, his arm hovering over his sheathed saber. “We exhausted all of the sooth during the journey. There was no time to replenish our supplies.” 

The silence that followed was heavy. White could feel the vibrations in the floorboards, a heartbeat before the impact. 

In an instant, the quiet shattered. A deafening crash roared as a carriage ripped through the makeshift barricade, flinging splintered debris across the room. Through the gaping breach, the dark mist spilled inside, twisting its tendrils of living dread, clinging to the floor and walls. 

With it, they came. Half a dozen malformed figures emerged, their pale skin pulled taut over grotesque, hunched frames. They trudged with unnatural, jerky movements on all fours—their limbs grated with every stride. 

White clenched his jaw as he recognised them; these were part of the soldiers he had slain at the outpost only days earlier. His focus narrowed on their approach.

“Hold fast!” The Commissar’s roar echoed, sending his men into a defensive wall, sabers ready to strike.

The ghoulish creatures bore down on them. His heart pounding against his ribs, White braced for the inevitable clash. Besides him, the thaumaturge’s fingers swiftly inscribed a sigil into the air; a second later, he unleashed a wave of scorching flames. 

The ghouls staggered under the sudden heat, their stride faltering. 

“Advance!” The Commissar’s command cut through the haze, spurring the men into action. They surged forward with lethal precision, blades flashing in the dim light as they tore into the twisted creatures still reeling from the fire. Three of the ghouls lay butchered on the floor while the survivors skittered back into the shadows.

Amid the chaos, White’s gaze locked onto a towering figure looming through the swirling mist. Without a word he broke formation. 

  
If he comes inside, Alana and the children might not survive. I must deal with him outside.

The Commissar roared for the men to hold their line, but the command was drowned out by an eruption of chaos breaking through the attic’s hatch. A koshmera—a stitched abomination of mismatched beasts—lunged from above into a frantic blur. 

It slammed into a soldier, catching him off guard. With a sickening crunch, the creature’s maw sank into his neck, stifling any scream. The man’s body went limp, swinging lifelessly in the koshmera’s grip.

White ignored the carnage behind him and ran through the breach into the street. There stood a tall figure, a Yfelkynn wielding a massive, rusted greatsword. A faint black shimmer pulsed across its skin, the giant’s moves were guided by black threads of lytel coming from somewhere within the mist. 

Within a breath, White sprang toward the cursed giant, his grip tight on the blade. Wrath gathered inside his chest.

The greatsword descended toward White, forcefully parting the mist in its path. He ducked beneath the arc, sliding forward through the grit. With one fluid motion, his dagger was buried deep in the foe’s heel. White felt the snap of a tendon and the giant’s form buckling under its own weight to one knee. 

Seizing the moment, White vaulted upward to deliver a final blow to the nape and end its misery.

Fuck, fuuuck … He winced in pain, a few tears escaping his closed eyelids. 

The steps stopped, the creature stood over him. Regret and sorrow welled up in his chest, regret that he fucked up and couldnt do more to protect the little ones he liberated. That he couldnt even protect Haroon. He wasnt even able to avenge his death. 

White expected the blow that would end him but none came. A pair of firm heavy hands grabbed him and hoisted him up. 

“You fool! Dont die now.”

He heard the Arcanist chanting and a boom resonated stifling the screeches. 

White felt the pressure of the broad shoulder under him, he was being carried. He could hear the awful bleat of pain fading away. 

“Quickly!” Yelled the Arcanist. “Get inside.”

  
  
  

+++The men moved to the sides, a half circle formed to flank him. The one on the right clenched his jaw and moved first, a swift jab with the spear. White dodged to the side and hooked the shaft while closing the distance. He kicked the shield, knocking the man out of balance making him slip on the cold mud. He felt the shift in the air behind him, as he spun around the second soldier was almost upon him. White threw the axe lodging it into the young man's skull. With a sudden groan he fell face first to the ground. 

The noble moved swiftly, capitalizing on the opportunity his men created. As White looked up, the noble moved his lips, dark red lytel flew from his tongue and wreathed the longsword with fire. He stepped forward to get under the slash, he was met with sharp pain. The first soldier recovered, and managed to skewer his thigh. White clenched his teeth, still keeping the liquid inside his mouth and pushed against the mud rolling to grab the sword from the ground. As he recovered his footing, he barely deflected the blow aimed at his head. 

White spat the liquid from his mouth, it ignited mid-air from the flaming sword. A violent hiss of fire burnt the enemy’s eyes. A silent scream drowned by the noise. The fire clung to his face, in agony he dropped his sword. White seized the moment and pulled out the dagger from his belt, plunging it in the noble's neck.
